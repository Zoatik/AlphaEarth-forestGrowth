# ICP Forests data
The project uses ICP Forests monitoring data. The numbered directories contain portal exports, and `icpf/03_tidy/` contains the processed datasets.

## Portal exports

An export directory follows the pattern `<user_id>_<survey_code>_<YYYYMMDDhhmmss>`. For example, `595_gr_20261112121624` is a growth survey export dated 12 November 2026. The timestamp identifies the export, while the survey years are recorded in the data. Each CSV name follows the pattern `<survey_code>_<form_code>.csv`.

| Code | Survey | Monitoring level |
|---|---|---|
| `cc` | Crown condition, including defoliation and damage | II |
| `dp` | Atmospheric deposition | II |
| `gr` | Tree and stand growth | II |
| `mm` | Meteorological measurements | II |
| `si` | Plot installation and stand description | II |
| `ss` | Soil solution chemistry | II |
| `y1` | Plot installation and stand description | I |

For growth analysis, `gr_ipm.csv` contains periodic measurements at tree level, `gr_inv.csv` contains periodic measurements at plot level, and `gr_pli.csv` describes the growth survey plots. The [`GR` form reference](https://icp-forests.org/documentation/Surveys/GR/index.html) and the [ICP Forests survey list](https://icp-forests.org/documentation/Surveys/index.html) describe the official codes. The exports also include column definitions in `adds/attribute_catalogue_<survey_code>.csv` where available.

Two reference CSVs sit alongside the exports. `gpd_level_ii.csv` includes Level II plot metadata, decimal coordinates (`lat_epsg4326`, `lon_epsg4326`), and survey flags. `tree_species_distribution.csv` lists species records by country, partner, and plot, with `from_year` and `to_year` fields.

## Cleaned Level II Parquet

File: `data/ICP/icpf/03_tidy/cpf-level2_cleaned.parquet`

**One row represents one growth period for one tree**, bounded by two diameter measurements. The current file has **39,922 rows and 110 columns**, covering 21,741 trees in 382 plots across 25 countries.

The combination of `tree_id`, `period_start`, and `period_end` is unique in this file. Use `plot_id` to group trees by plot. `survey_year` ranges from 1997 to 2017 and can differ from the calendar year of `period_end`. Use the actual measurement dates when aligning observations with annual satellite data.

| Columns | Description |
|---|---|
| `tree_id`, `plot_id`, `country`, `specie` and associated codes | Tree, plot, country, and species identifiers or labels |
| `period_start`, `period_end`, `period_duration_d`, `period_duration_y` | Measurement dates and elapsed time in days and years |
| `diameter_start`, `diameter_end`, `growth*` | Diameters in centimetres and absolute or relative growth over the period |
| `diameter_method_*`, `diameter_quality_end`, `removal_*` | Measurement method, quality, and removal information |
| `plot_latitude`, `plot_longitude`, `plot_altitude`, `plot_slope`, `plot_orientation` | Plot location and terrain |
| `num_defoliation_obs`, `defoliation_*`, `social_class_*`, `was_*` | Observation count and summaries of defoliation and tree social status |
| `num_deposition_obs`, `yearly_precip`, `dep_*` | Deposition observation count, precipitation, and deposition chemistry |
| `num_soil_obs`, `ss_*` | Soil solution observation count and chemistry |
| `soph_avg_sdi`, `soph_avg_age`, `soph_avg_temp`, `soph_avg_precip` | Average stand density index, stand age, temperature, and precipitation |

The growth fields use the following formulas:

| Field | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| `growth` | `diameter_end - diameter_start` | cm |
| `growth_rate` | `growth / period_duration_y` | cm/year |
| `growth_rel` | `growth / diameter_start` | Fraction |
| `growth_rate_rel` | `growth_rel / period_duration_y` | Fraction/year |

For example, an increase from 37.2 cm to 39.2 cm over 4.05 years gives an annual growth rate of about 0.49 cm/year. The growth fields are populated throughout the file. Coverage varies for the environmental variables. For example, all four `soph_avg_*` columns are missing on 22,854 rows.

The plot coordinates are WGS84 degrees, minutes, and seconds stored as signed integers. For example, `473400` represents 47°34′00″, or about 47.5667°. Convert the coordinates to decimal degrees before using them in Earth Engine, and pass longitude before latitude when constructing a point. Treat coordinate precision separately from the resolution of the satellite data.
